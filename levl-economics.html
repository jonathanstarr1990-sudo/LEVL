Revenue Model
Recommended model: tiered SaaS with a marketplace layer at scale.
Not transaction fees at launch — too hard to track, too much legal complexity, creates friction at the point where adoption matters most. Subscriptions first. Marketplace when there is deal flow liquidity.

Three user types, three distinct pricing structures. Creator volume drives the network effect. Manager and brand subscriptions drive the P&L. Marketplace facilitation is the long-term upside that makes the model compelling to investors.

Investor Questions — Prepared Answers
How do you solve the cold start problem?
You need creators before sponsors will pay, and sponsors before creators see value. The answer is manager-led onboarding: managers sign up and bring their entire roster. One Agency signup = 10–30 creator profiles on the platform immediately. Founding 100 and direct creator outreach handle the supply side. For demand, start with smaller agencies and indie brands — not enterprise — where sales cycles are shorter and willingness to try new tooling is higher.
What stops YouTube or TikTok from building this themselves?
Platform risk is real and should be acknowledged honestly. The answer is that LEVL is cross-platform by design. YouTube verifying reputation only on YouTube is useless to a sponsor who needs a creator active across YouTube, Twitch, and Instagram. The value is aggregation and portability across platform relationships — something no single platform has an incentive to provide because it would benefit competitors. LEVL is infrastructure that sits above the platforms, not inside them.
What is your long-term take rate?
SaaS subscriptions as the base, facilitation fee on deal flow as the growth layer. At scale: subscription ARR in the £2–5M range; facilitation revenue potentially exceeding subscription revenue as deal volume grows. We will not try to charge both a subscription and a transaction fee for the same user action — the risk is too high at this stage and creates friction at the wrong moment.
What is the defensibility? Why can't someone copy this?
The data moat. Once a creator's 3-year verified history is in LEVL, rebuilding it elsewhere requires starting from scratch. Verified signals compound over time — a creator with a 4-year record is more valuable on LEVL than on any new entrant without that history. The reputation graph also gets more valuable with every participant added. A competitor launching 18 months later is not building the same product — they are building an empty version of it.
What does the Instagram API restriction do to your model?
It is a real constraint. At launch: creator-verified screenshots plus counterparty confirmation, flagged clearly as "counterparty-confirmed" rather than "API-verified." This preserves honesty and does not break the verification framework — it just makes Instagram signals slightly lower confidence. The plan is a Meta partnership conversation at Series A when we have meaningful user numbers. TikTok has similar restrictions and the same approach applies.
What is your 18-month ARR target?
Approximately £900K–1M ARR: 2,000 Creator Pro at £144/year, 200 Manager Agency at £2,388/year, 50 Brand Discovery at £3,588/year. This assumes a £500–750K seed round closed in Q3 2026 and a 6-person team by month 12. At that ARR level the business is approaching Series A range. Any marketplace revenue is upside on this projection.
Why is the creator tier so cheap?
Because creator volume is the product, not the revenue. Creators being on the platform is what makes the platform valuable to managers and brands. Charging creators a high price would slow adoption and damage the network effect at the exact stage where network effect is the only thing that matters. £12/month is a rounding error relative to a single brand deal — but it validates intent and covers infrastructure cost at scale.
